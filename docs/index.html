Negative result · September 2026

The text encoder isn't doing anything.

Aster asks whether encoding a biological question as language buys generalization to questions a model was never trained on. On PeptiVerse, it does not — and the reason is structural, not a matter of tuning.

Repository: sathvikask0/aster · Dataset: PeptiVerse (ChatterjeeLab) · Backbone: ESM-2 8M + MiniLM-L6-v2 · Currently running →

The central result

Trained on all three properties, tested on questions it has seen

The architecture pairs a protein encoder with a text encoder and scores their outputs by dot product. Swap the text tower for a lookup table over three integers and nothing changes.

A frozen 384-dimensional sentence encoder performs identically to three integers. Whatever the model learned, it did not learn it from the words.

Why this can't be fixed by tuning

With three tasks, the question input takes exactly three distinct values. A 384-dimensional vector restricted to three distinct values carries the same information as a three-way one-hot — not approximately, identically. dual and task_id are not competing hypotheses here; they are the same function written two ways. The experiment is unidentifiable at this scale.

Every experiment, in order

Including the ones that failed, and the ones that failed for interesting reasons

01 Unusable Arc Institute virtual cell sample
600 cells, 5 target genes, 1,000 measured genes. Label counts came out 1 / 4,997 / 2 across decrease / no-change / increase. 99.94% of labels say "no change", so always answering "no change" scores 99.94%. Five target genes also means five questions — too few to hold one out. Fine for testing plumbing, which is what it was used for.
02 Passed Synthetic control rig — semantic regime
Questions built compositionally from bound parts, so an unseen question is a new combination of familiar components. Held-out questions: dual +0.962 lift, task_id −0.014. Transfer works when the text genuinely carries the rule.
03 Passed Synthetic control rig — arbitrary regime
Identical setup, but questions are opaque identifiers and the decision rule is drawn independently of them. Chance is the ceiling by construction. dual scored −0.093. The harness reports no transfer where no transfer is possible. This is the check that makes every other number in the rig worth reading.
04 Finding Random splits cannot discriminate the hypothesis
On a random split, dual scored +0.992 and task_id +0.982 — indistinguishable. Any result reported on a random split is consistent with the text encoder doing nothing at all. This predicted the real-data outcome before the real data arrived.
05 Expectation was wrong Covariate shift creates a real entity-only shortcut
The rig pre-registered "entity-only sits at chance everywhere". Under family shift it scored +0.204. Not a bug. Held-out families far from the origin in latent space have their labels dominated by the family offset, so per-entity label agreement across questions reaches 0.63 and ignoring the question becomes a genuine strategy. The threshold was not loosened; the shortcut is now measured per split and every claim is a margin over it.
06 Clean PeptiVerse data audit
Four of seven tasks carry amino-acid sequences: hemolysis (6,073), solubility (18,453), nephrotoxicity (17,180), penetrance (2,324). All 100% parseable, labels already binary, zero duplicates, zero train/validation leakage. Toxicity, Caco2 and PAMPA are SMILES-only, so ESM-2 cannot read them without a chemical encoder.
07 Tasks not independent Solubility negatives are a subset of nephrotoxicity
All 9,668 solubility negatives appear in nephrotoxicity, labelled 0 in both, with zero exceptions — 52% of the solubility task. Training on one while holding out the other makes half the test set answerable by recall. Dropping the shared rows leaves solubility single-class, so the rows cannot be salvaged: nephrotoxicity is dropped as a task instead.
08 Finding Chance is not the baseline
Amino-acid composition alone scores 0.784 on penetrance against a 0.500 majority class. Hemolysis 0.784, solubility 0.583. Every subsequent number is reported as a margin over these, never over chance. Hemolysis is the interesting one — composition buys nothing there, so it is a genuinely hard task where a real model could show its worth.
09 No transfer Leave-one-task-out on real peptides
ESM-2 8M embeddings, frozen MiniLM text encoder, class-weighted loss. Every cell below its shortcut ceiling; hemolysis landed at 0.246 against a 0.784 ceiling. Uninterpretable on its own — nothing yet established the model could learn a question it had seen.
10 Decisive In-distribution control
dual 0.715, task_id 0.718. The model learns each property well, and the text encoder contributes nothing. This is the result. It confirms on real data exactly what experiment 04 predicted from synthetic data.

How the evaluation is built

The controls exist to explain away the hypothesis, not to flatter it.

  • task_id — replaces the text encoder with a lookup table whose unknown row is zero-initialized and never trained, so an unseen question yields chance structurally rather than empirically. This is the transfer floor.
  • composition — amino-acid frequencies instead of ESM-2, to test whether the protein model earns its cost.
  • question_only / entity_only — never see one input. Whatever they score is prior, not biology.

Vocabularies are built from training data only; letting test tokens in would hand unseen questions a trained embedding and manufacture transfer. Temperature is fit on a validation set carved from training, never on test. The text encoder stays frozen — with two training tasks, fine-tuning it would teach it their wording and destroy the general semantics the held-out task depends on.

That is the wrong zero, and the ceiling it was compared against was additionally fitted on the same rows it scored. Both are fixed below. The verdict changes from resolution to half a resolution. Since then the cross-attention model has also been rebuilt to score the candidate answers it was ignoring, so the model accuracies on this page are version-1 numbers awaiting a re-run.

The negative result above established that 3 atomic questions are unidentifiable from a 3-entry lookup table. In v0.2, Aster scales to 21 distinct microbial target assays curated from 7 public databases (64,602 associations) with 5 held-out targets spanning Gram-negative, Gram-positive, and fungal membrane barriers.

Questions were reformulated from atomic names to mechanistic biophysical prompts (specifying LPS outer membrane disruption, thick peptidoglycan penetration, and chitin/ergosterol binding), and the rigid pooled dot product was replaced with Multi-Head Cross-Attention (CrossAttentionAster) allowing prompt tokens to query peptide feature subspaces.

Two zeros, and they disagree

task_id is the lookup floor — structurally chance on an unseen task. Beating it says the text tower is no longer a lookup table. ceiling is max(majority, composition probe), where the probe is a logistic regression on 20-dimensional amino-acid frequencies scored out of fold. Beating it says the model knows something a bag of amino acids does not. That is the real zero.

Experiments completed

Every run so far, and what each one settled

Experiment Verdict What it settled
Arc virtual cell sample Unusable 99.94% of labels are “no change” and 5 target genes means 5 questions. Plumbing test only.
Synthetic control rig Passed 16/16 pre-registered checks. The harness transfers when transfer is possible (+0.96) and not when it isn't (−0.09). The instrument works.
Entity-only shortcut discovery Expectation was wrong A pre-registered expectation failed at +0.204. Shortcut level is now measured per split instead of assumed.
PeptiVerse, 3 tasks (v0.1) Decisive dual ≈ task_id. Three questions in a 384-d space are 2 degrees of freedom; the text encoder became an expensive one-hot.
AMP multi-task, 21 assays (v0.2) Half a resolution Lookup floor cleared (+0.054). Composition ceiling not cleared (mean −0.058, 1/5 targets above, and that one inside the intervals).

Audit of the controls themselves Two were broken The cross-attention model never read the candidate answers — it classified into fixed slots, so the answer text was unused and the option count was stuck at two. And entity_only, the control that is supposed to detect “answers without reading the question”, scored every option identically, so it emitted one answer for every row: it was measuring the label prior. On the split where the rig famously caught a shortcut at 0.602, “always answer option 0” scores exactly 0.6021. Both fixed; the rig still passes 16/16, and the repaired control raises the measured ceiling on one split by 0.13.

Every number above is measured against a ceiling high enough to swallow the effect being measured. Positives are real antimicrobial peptides; negatives are peptides with no record for that target, drawn at random. The two pools differ in bulk composition, so “is this peptide cationic and amphipathic?” answers most of the benchmark without reading the prompt — on C. albicans, to 0.721.

The fix is not a better model. It is a benchmark where the shortcut does not exist. Each positive claims its nearest unused negative in composition-and-length space, greedily, with a caliper — so a bag-of-amino-acids probe is at chance by construction, and anything left must come from residue order and prompt semantics.

Experiments of the future

Queued, in the order they would change the most

01 Queued Data with measured inactives
Every negative in this project is presumed: the source tables record reported activity and never recorded inactivity, so a negative means “no record”, which is partly a statement about what anyone assayed. Matching removes a confound; it does not create a measured negative. The clean answer needs MIC tables with upper bounds rather than lists of hits.
02 Queued Seed variance and split variance
Every number published so far is one seed on one split, with binomial intervals that ignore peptide-level correlation. Before any lift is called real it needs to survive re-drawing both, and the held-out targets need to rotate rather than stay fixed at five.
03 Queued Does a larger protein backbone clear the ceiling?
Everything here runs on ESM-2 8M. If matched negatives leave only residue-order signal, backbone capacity becomes the binding constraint, and 650M is the obvious next rung. Cheap to test, and it cleanly separates “the premise is wrong” from “the encoder is too small.”
04 Queued Calibration and abstention under shift
On unseen questions the v0.1 models were confidently wrong — expected calibration error of 0.22 to 0.32, overconfidence positive everywhere. For a model whose purpose is deciding what to test next, that is the failure that matters most. An abstention rule has to be validated under distribution shift; entropy alone is not evidence that a model knows when it is wrong.
05 Queued Back to cells, with a real perturbation cohort
The original first capability — will reducing one gene's activity raise or lower another gene's RNA? — is still untested, because the 600-cell Arc sample is 99.94% one label. It needs the full atlas or the filtered Replogle set, both of which need a licensing and provenance audit before a single training run.
06 Queued Conjunctive questions, where the data allows
Compositional questions are what made the synthetic regime transfer, and PeptiVerse could not supply them: task overlap is 186 peptides for hemolysis and penetrance, and 2 for hemolysis and solubility. The multi-pathogen data has the overlap that PeptiVerse lacked, so conjunctions across targets are now formable — and they are the sharpest available test of whether the text tower composes rather than memorizes.
